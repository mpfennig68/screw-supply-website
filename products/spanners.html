Two-Hole Spanner Security Screws

Two-hole spanner screws use two holes positioned in the head as the drive interface. A matching spanner bit or driver uses two pins or prongs that engage those holes — common flat, Phillips, hex, and ordinary six-lobe drivers don't provide the intended engagement. The matching tool allows normal installation and authorized removal, which is why the drive is used where casual access or removal should be discouraged.

// HOW THE DRIVE WORKS

How two-hole spanner security drives work

Two holes in the fastener head form the drive interface. A compatible spanner tool has two pins or prongs sized and spaced to fit into those holes, transferring installation or removal torque through both engagement points at once.

Correct spacing and tool size are necessary for proper engagement — full, even contact at both holes helps reduce slipping and damage to the drive during use.

The drive discourages casual removal with common tools, but it remains tamper-resistant rather than removal-proof. Unlike a one-way screw, a spanner screw is designed for authorized removal with the matching tool, not just one-time installation.

// SPECIFYING A SCREW

How to specify a two-hole spanner security screw

Machine screws are generally used with a tapped hole or nut, while sheet-metal screws form or engage threads directly in the receiving material. Flat heads are normally selected where a countersunk, flush installation is required; pan and oval heads give different installed profiles. The matching spanner tool must correspond to the hole spacing and drive size, and environmental and compatibility requirements should be reviewed for your specific application. Confirm the following, and include as much as possible on any RFQ:

  • Screw formMachine screw or sheet-metal screw
  • DiameterNominal screw diameter
  • Thread type / pitchMachine thread size or sheet-metal thread type
  • LengthOverall or thread length

  • Material18-8 stainless steel

// MATCHING TOOLS

Match the spanner tool to the fastener

Spanner bits and drivers use two pins or prongs to engage the head — tool size and pin spacing must match the fastener, and a mismatched or poorly seated tool can slip or damage the engagement holes. Order or confirm the necessary tooling with the screws; not every security kit contains every spanner size.

// FAQ

Common questions

What is a two-hole spanner security screw?
It's a security screw with two holes in the head that form the drive interface, engaged by a matching spanner tool with two pins or prongs sized to fit those holes.
Can an ordinary screwdriver remove a spanner screw?
No. Flat, Phillips, hex, and ordinary six-lobe drivers do not engage the two-hole drive. Normal installation and authorized removal require a matching spanner bit or driver.
How do I select the correct spanner bit or driver?
The tool's pin spacing and size must match the fastener's drive. A mismatched or poorly seated tool can slip or damage the engagement holes, so confirm sizing before ordering.
Which screw forms, head styles, and sizes are available?
Available forms include machine screws and sheet-metal screws, generally in pan, flat, and oval head styles, in sizes from #4 through 1/4 inch. Exact availability varies and should be confirmed when requesting a quote.
What material is offered for spanner security screws?
The verified material is 18-8 stainless steel. Confirm current availability when requesting a quote.

Yes. We can help confirm matching spanner bits and drivers, and review custom fastener requirements. Send your details through a quote request and we will follow up.
